# KES Website Blueprint – Version 1

**Project:** Kluthe Environmental Solutions, LLC (KES) website redesign  
**Status:** Stage 1 strategy and homepage structure substantially complete  
**Primary build direction:** Static website developed collaboratively in GitHub  
**Primary business goal:** Encourage the right prospective client to contact KES by making them feel understood, building trust, and making the next step feel easy and natural.

---

## 1. Core Website Goal

The KES website should help a prospective client recognize that KES understands their problem, trust KES to help solve it, and feel comfortable starting a conversation.

The website is not intended to be a hard-sales platform. Its role is professional-services marketing: helping the right visitor think:

> These people understand what I am dealing with. They know this work. They seem practical and easy to work with. I should contact them.

The desired conversion is simple:

> I should talk to John.

---

## 2. Primary Visitor

### Desired primary visitor

A farmer or landowner who knows they have a problem or project but may not know exactly what professional service or conservation assistance they need.

### Typical primary visitor today

A farmer or landowner who has already worked with NRCS, entered into a conservation contract, and now needs an experienced professional to help them understand the requirements and successfully carry the project through.

### Final audience statement

> Farmers and landowners facing an agricultural conservation, natural-resource, or farm-planning challenge—whether they are exploring solutions independently or already participating in a USDA/NRCS conservation program.

### Design priority

> Meet visitors where they are. Do not require them to know the name of the plan, practice, program, or technical deliverable they need.

The website should support the business KES has today while helping KES grow beyond dependence on NRCS-originated referrals.

---

## 3. Visitor Journey

The visitor should move through the homepage emotionally and logically:

1. **Arrival:** “Do these people do what I need?”
2. **Recognition:** “Yes. That is actually the problem I am dealing with.”
3. **Relief:** “Okay. There are ways to deal with this.”
4. **Confidence:** “These people understand agriculture and conservation programs.”
5. **Trust:** “They seem practical, experienced, and straightforward.”
6. **Personal connection:** “I can see who I am going to be working with.”
7. **Action:** “I will contact John and talk it through.”

### Core principle

> Every section should earn the visitor’s attention for the next section—and give them a reason to want to keep going.

Each section should do at least one of three things:

- Help the visitor recognize themselves or their situation.
- Reduce uncertainty.
- Increase trust.

If a section does none of those, it probably does not belong on the homepage.

---

## 4. What the Visitor Should Understand in the First 15–30 Seconds

The visitor should quickly understand five things:

### 1. KES works with people like me

The site should immediately feel agricultural and grounded in working farms and working lands.

### 2. KES understands the system I am dealing with

NRCS should be visible early enough to reassure current referral-based clients, but it should not dominate the KES identity.

### 3. KES understands the farm, not just the paperwork

The website should communicate that KES understands agronomy, conservation planning, livestock and nutrient management, field conditions, and implementation—not just forms and deliverables.

### 4. KES will help me figure this out

The visitor should feel that they do not need to understand all the acronyms, contract items, or technical deliverables before contacting KES.

### 5. These are people I would be comfortable calling

The tone should communicate:

- Experienced
- Approachable
- Practical
- Responsive
- Calm
- Organized

### Working summary statement

> KES helps farmers and landowners solve conservation and farm-planning challenges. They understand agriculture, NRCS programs, and the technical requirements behind them. They help clients understand what they need, develop practical solutions for the operation, and move through the process with confidence.

## 5. Brand Positioning and Personality

### Primary positioning principle

> The client is the hero. KES is the experienced guide.

The client’s farm, problem, goals, and decisions remain at the center of the story. KES earns trust by demonstrating understanding, technical competence, and practical guidance.

### KES should feel

- Practical
- Experienced
- Approachable
- Agricultural
- Independent
- Technically credible
- Calm
- Personal

### KES should not feel

- Corporate
- Bureaucratic
- Salesy
- Trendy
- Overly polished
- Governmental
- Environmental-activist

### Balance

- **Fairly personal**
- **NRCS visible but not dominant**
- **Approximately 60% trusted farm adviser / 40% technical consultant**

### Core emotional promise

> This may be complicated, but it is manageable.

and

> You are going to work with people who understand the process and will help you through it.

## 6. Voice and Writing Style

The website should sound like a knowledgeable conservation professional speaking plainly across the kitchen table.

### Writing should be

- Clear
- Direct
- Human
- Practical
- Technically accurate
- Client-centered

### Avoid

- Corporate jargon
- Vague sustainability language
- Excessive technical terminology before the visitor understands why it matters
- Long explanatory blocks on the homepage
- Sales pressure

### Content rule

> Speak first in the client’s language. Introduce professional and NRCS terminology only after the visitor understands why it matters.

Examples:

- “Manure storage and runoff concerns” before “CNMP / CEMA-226 / engineering coordination.”
- “Trying to improve soil performance” before “CPA-116 / DIA-162.”

### Key working phrase

> You don’t need to know what plan you need. Start with what you’re trying to accomplish.

This is a strong candidate for use on the website and should remain part of the project vocabulary even if the final wording changes.

## 7. Visual Direction

### Overall feeling

Warm, spacious, agricultural, credible, restrained, and real.

### Photography

Real KES/farm photography should do the emotional work.

Preferred subjects:

- Working farms
- Pasture and livestock
- Cropland
- Farmsteads
- Conservation practices
- Site visits
- Soil sampling
- Planning discussions
- Maps and field work
- Natural client/collaborator interactions

Real and imperfect is preferable to generic stock photography.

### Graphic language

KES’s gray agricultural silhouettes should be used as secondary visual elements, not as the primary emotional imagery.

Potential uses:

- Section dividers
- Service-page banners
- Quiet background accents
- Edge treatments
- Header/footer accents

### Color direction

Use a restrained palette built around existing KES branding:

- White or warm off-white backgrounds
- Charcoal or dark gray text
- One muted agricultural green
- Possibly one secondary earth tone

Avoid bright “eco green” and overly colorful environmental branding.

### Typography

Simple, highly readable, and professional.

Likely approach:

- One strong sans-serif family
- Large, clear headings
- Short line lengths
- Generous spacing
- Minimal typographic ornament

### Motion and interaction

Keep animation minimal.

The visitor should be pulled forward by:

> Recognition + clarity + authenticity + trust

—not by sliders, popups, video backgrounds, or visual gimmicks.

**Status:** Structure approved; wording remains open to refinement during development.

---

### Section 2 — Recognition

**Purpose:** “They understand what I am dealing with.”

**Working heading:**

> What are you trying to accomplish on your farm?

**Recognizable situations may include:**

- Expanding a livestock operation
- Managing manure, runoff, or water-quality concerns
- Improving soil or crop performance
- Planning grazing improvements
- Considering organic production
- Dealing with weeds, insects, or crop-health concerns
- Already have an NRCS contract and are not sure what comes next

Each item should be short, highly scannable, and written in client language.

**Anchor message:**

> You don’t need to know what plan you need. Start with what you’re trying to accomplish.

**Visual direction:** Open, bright, simple blocks or cards; no wall of technical service names.

**Status:** Structurally approved.

---

### Section 3 — Reassurance / KES Can Help You Sort This Out

**Purpose:** “They can help me make sense of this.”

**Working heading:**

> Conservation Projects Can Get Complicated Quickly

**Core message:**

Funding programs, technical requirements, engineering, nutrient management, conservation practices, and implementation schedules often have to fit together. KES helps clients understand what is required, evaluate practical options, and develop a clear path forward.

**NRCS callout:**

> Already have an NRCS contract?
>
> We can help you understand what you’ve contracted for, what information is needed, and how the planning and implementation pieces fit together.

**Visual direction:** Calmer than Section 2; more breathing room; one strong photo of planning, mapping, or a client discussion would fit well.

**Status:** Structurally approved.

---

### Section 4 — Services

**Purpose:** “They do the kind of work I need.”

Services should be organized into understandable families rather than a wall of NRCS acronyms.

#### Livestock & Nutrient Management

Client-facing cue:

> Managing manure, livestock facilities, or nutrient concerns?

Potential services:

- Comprehensive Nutrient Management Plans
- Nutrient Management
- Site Suitability / Waste Storage planning support
- Heavy-use and production-area planning
- Engineering coordination

#### Soil & Crop Management

Client-facing cue:

> Trying to improve soil performance or change how your cropping system is managed?

Potential services:

- Soil Health Management Plans
- Pest Management Conservation System Design
- Transition to Organic Planning
- Nutrient-use and agronomic planning

#### Conservation Planning

Client-facing cue:

> Need a broader plan for how the pieces of your farm fit together?

Potential services:

- Whole-farm conservation planning
- Grazing management
- Erosion and resource-concern planning
- Land-treatment planning
- Practice selection and implementation sequencing

#### Technical & Project Support

Client-facing cue:

> Already have a conservation project underway and need help moving it forward?

Potential services:

- NRCS/TSP technical deliverables
- Contract interpretation and project coordination
- Mapping and field evaluation
- Technical documentation
- Coordination with engineers and specialists

**Visual direction:** Four clean cards in a 2×2 desktop arrangement; stack on mobile.

**Status:** Structurally approved.

---

### Section 5 — How We Work

**Purpose:** “This sounds practical and manageable.”

Keep this section intentionally brief.

#### Understand the Operation

> We start with your farm, your goals, and the problem you’re trying to solve.

#### Evaluate the Options

> We look at the technical requirements, practical alternatives, and available programs.

#### Develop a Workable Plan

> We bring the pieces together into a clear path forward that fits the operation.

**Working supporting line:**

> The plan should serve the farm—not the other way around.

**Visual direction:** Simple three-step progression; not a heavy corporate process graphic.

**Status:** Structurally approved; may be expanded slightly during development if the actual page needs it.

---

### Section 6 — Why KES

**Purpose:** “I trust these people to help me.”

**Layout:** Two-column section.

**Left side:**

- One strong real photograph of John working in a farm/conservation setting

**Right side:**

- Short heading
- Concise KES story
- Compact credibility strip

**Working heading options:**

> Experience That Helps Make the Process Clearer

or

> Agricultural Experience. Conservation Expertise. Practical Guidance.

Current preference: the first option because it stays focused on the client benefit.

**Working story direction:**

KES was built around the idea that conservation planning should help farmers make better decisions, not add another layer of confusion. John’s experience in agronomy, conservation planning, USDA-NRCS programs, nutrient management, and farm-level resource concerns helps connect technical project requirements with the realities of the operation itself.

**Potential credibility markers:**

- NRCS Certified Conservation Planner
- NRCS Technical Service Provider
- Certified Crop Adviser
- B.S. Agronomy / M.S.
- Former NRCS Conservationist
- Multi-state agricultural consulting

**NRCS framing principle:**

> Experience from inside the conservation system, now brought directly to farmers and landowners.

**Status:** Structurally approved. John already has a photograph in mind for this section.

---

### Section 7 — Proof / Testimonials

**Purpose:** “Other people in situations like mine trusted KES and were glad they did.”

The homepage should feature a small number of strong testimonials, likely three.

Select testimonials to prove different parts of the KES promise:

1. KES understood the farm and the client’s goals.
2. KES made a complicated process understandable.
3. KES was practical, responsive, and good to work with.

Prefer 1–3 sentences per testimonial on the homepage.

Use client name, farm/business name, and state where permission exists.

**Visual direction:** Clean testimonial cards with generous white space. Avoid oversized decorative quote graphics.

**Status:** Structurally approved. John has spent the summer collecting testimonial material for review during development.

---

### Section 8 — The People Behind KES

**Purpose:** “I know who I am dealing with.”

KES should not be presented as a family team or a large firm.

Current structure:

> **John Kluthe — Principal Consultant**  
> John is the main client contact and leads the planning work.

Then:

> **Engineering & Technical Collaborators**  
> KES works with trusted independent engineers and other specialists when a project requires additional technical expertise.

Matt Brubaker may be identified as a key engineering collaborator. Additional collaborators can be added as those working relationships become established.

**Core positioning:**

> KES stays intentionally small and personal while bringing in specialized expertise when the project requires it.

**Status:** Conceptually finalized.

**Purpose:** “I am ready to start a conversation.”

**Working heading:**

## 10. Photography Plan

KES should build a small, intentional photo library rather than collect random images.

### Priority photo needs

- 1–2 strong landscape / hero photos
- 2–3 “KES at work” photos
- 2–3 conservation-practice photos
- 1–2 people / collaboration photos
- 1–2 detail photos
- Simple natural portraits where needed

### Photo guidance

- Shoot horizontal more often than vertical.
- Leave visual breathing room around the subject.
- Avoid cropping too tightly.
- Take multiple versions of promising scenes.
- Capture both whole-setting and detail views.
- Real working farms are preferable to “perfect” farm imagery.

## 13. Build and Workflow Direction

Use GitHub for the new KES website development process.

The website is expected to be largely static and primarily designed to encourage prospective clients to contact KES.

### Working philosophy

Use as little technology as necessary.

Likely characteristics:

- Static site
- GitHub-based source control
- Version history
- Branches for experimentation
- Preview before publishing
- Minimal maintenance burden

### Desired workflow

> Idea → Build → Preview → John reviews → Revise → Approve → Publish

The live KES website should not be disturbed until the replacement is ready and approved.

## 16. Guiding Questions for Future Review

When evaluating future design or copy decisions, ask:

1. Does this help the visitor recognize their situation?
2. Does this reduce uncertainty?
3. Does this increase trust?
4. Is the client still the hero and KES the guide?
5. Are we speaking in the client’s language before using technical terminology?
6. Does this feel practical, agricultural, experienced, and approachable?
7. Does this make the visitor want to continue?
8. Does this move the visitor closer to a natural conversation with KES?

If the answer is no, the element should be reconsidered.
